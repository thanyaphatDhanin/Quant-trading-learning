# Learning Roadmap

The Quant Trading Learning Platform is organized into five learning areas. Together, they introduce the financial concepts, research methods, systematic trading principles, programming skills, and AI tools used in quantitative trading.

The curriculum is designed for independent learners and progresses from foundational concepts to practical research and strategy evaluation.

## 1. Financial Literacy

Understand the basic concepts and mechanics of financial markets.

**Initial topics**

- Financial Markets and Market Participants
- Basic Stock Market Terminology
- Bid, Ask, and Spread
- Market Orders and Limit Orders
- Trading Volume and Liquidity
- Trading Costs
- Gross Returns vs. Net Returns

## 2. Quant Research

Learn how to formulate research questions about financial markets and evaluate potential patterns using data.

**Initial topics**

- Introduction to Quantitative Research
- Descriptive Statistics
- Probability and Expected Value
- Returns and Volatility
- Correlation
- Introduction to Time Series
- Alpha Fundamentals
- Trading Edge Fundamentals
- Signal and Noise
- Statistical Evidence

**Research principle:** A pattern observed in historical data is a hypothesis to investigate, not proof of a persistent trading advantage.

## 3. Quant Trading System

Understand how a research idea can be translated into systematic trading rules and evaluated using historical data.

**Initial topics**

- Strategy Rules and Trading Signals
- Entry and Exit Conditions
- Holding Period
- Introduction to Backtesting
- Basic Performance Measurement
- Introduction to Risk and Drawdown
- Transaction Costs
- Basic Strategy Validation

As the curriculum develops, this area will expand to cover out-of-sample testing, robustness analysis, portfolio construction, position sizing, and execution quality.

## 4. Programming for Quant

Develop programming skills through simple examples and financial data exercises.

**Initial topics**

- Introduction to Programming
- Python Fundamentals
- Working with Tables and Data
- Introduction to NumPy and pandas
- Basic Data Cleaning
- Calculating Returns
- Creating Simple Charts
- Implementing Basic Research Calculations

Programming exercises will be introduced progressively. Learners can begin with conceptual examples and small calculations before working with more code.

Programming supports the research process; it does not replace statistical reasoning or sound research design.

## 5. AI for Quant Research

Learn how AI tools can support independent learning and research while understanding their limitations.

**Initial topics**

- Introduction to Large Language Models (LLMs)
- Writing Clear Prompts
- Using AI to Understand Technical Concepts
- Research Assistance
- AI-Assisted Coding
- Checking Sources and Verifying Claims
- Understanding Hallucinations and Limitations
- Responsible Use of AI in Research

AI-generated explanations, calculations, and code must be checked rather than accepted without verification. Important research results should be independently validated against reliable sources, data, and reproducible calculations.

## Recommended Learning Sequence

The five learning areas are connected rather than strictly sequential. A practical starting sequence is:

1. **Understand the market** — Learn basic financial terminology, market mechanics, liquidity, and trading costs.
2. **Build research foundations** — Study probability, statistics, returns, volatility, correlation, and time series.
3. **Explore Alpha and Trading Edge** — Form hypotheses, distinguish signals from noise, and evaluate statistical evidence.
4. **Learn programming progressively** — Use Python and data tools to reproduce calculations and analyze financial data.
5. **Develop systematic strategies** — Define trading rules, backtest hypotheses, and evaluate performance.
6. **Validate results and assess risk** — Examine overfitting, out-of-sample performance, drawdown, and transaction costs.
7. **Use AI responsibly** — Apply AI to learning and research while independently checking its outputs.

Learners may study programming alongside research fundamentals rather than waiting until they have completed the earlier areas.

## Learning Principles

- Evidence before conclusions.
- Research questions before strategy rules.
- Reproducible calculations over unsupported claims.
- Explicit assumptions and limitations.
- Statistical evidence does not guarantee future performance.
- Transaction costs and risk are integral to strategy evaluation.
- Historical backtest results do not guarantee live trading results.
- AI supports research; it does not replace independent verification.

## Curriculum Development

This roadmap represents the initial structure of the platform. Topics, exercises, research templates, and supporting references will be added progressively.

The initial phase prioritizes educational documentation, research methodology, and accessible learning materials. More advanced programming exercises, research tools, and external integrations may be introduced in later phases.
