# Quant Trading Learning

**An Accelerated, Self-Directed Education Platform for Quantitative Trading**

Learn independently. Research systematically. Validate with evidence.

## About This Project

Quant Trading Learning is an open-source educational project designed to help independent learners understand quantitative trading from the ground up.

The project provides accessible explanations, simple examples, and practical exercises to help learners gradually build their understanding of financial markets, quantitative research, statistics, and programming.

The goal is to make quantitative trading knowledge easier to access, understand, and practice — especially for people who are starting from zero.

Learners can study independently, work through examples, complete exercises, and develop their knowledge at their own pace.

The project welcomes both Thai-speaking learners and the international quantitative finance community.

## Who Is This Project For?

This project is for people who:

- Are interested in quantitative trading but do not know where to start.
- Have little or no programming experience.
- Want to understand how mathematics, statistics, and data can be applied to financial markets.
- Prefer learning through simple explanations, examples, and practical exercises.
- Want to develop their own research skills instead of relying solely on trading signals or unsupported claims.

**You do not need to know everything before you begin.**

No prior coding experience or advanced mathematics is required to get started. The learning materials aim to introduce concepts progressively, with opportunities to practice and verify what you learn.

What matters is curiosity, a willingness to learn, and the discipline to examine ideas using evidence.

## Learning Materials

The project focuses on three types of educational resources.

### 1. Concepts and Explanations

Clear explanations of quantitative trading concepts, written to help beginners understand what a concept means, why it matters, and how it relates to systematic trading.

Examples of topics include:

- What Is Quantitative Trading?
- What Is an Alpha Signal?
- What Is a Trading Edge?
- Signal vs. Noise
- Probability and Expected Value
- Correlation and Statistical Evidence
- Overfitting and Out-of-Sample Testing
- Transaction Costs and Net Returns

### 2. Simple Examples

Small, practical examples that demonstrate how quantitative concepts work.

Examples may include:

- Calculating asset returns.
- Comparing the returns of different stocks.
- Calculating averages and volatility.
- Measuring correlations between variables.
- Evaluating a simple trading signal.
- Comparing hypothetical strategy returns before and after transaction costs.

Examples are intended to make abstract concepts easier to understand before learners move on to more complex applications.

### 3. Practical Exercises

Exercises that allow learners to apply what they have learned and check their understanding.

Each exercise should include, where appropriate:

- **Objective:** What the learner should understand or accomplish.
- **Instructions:** What to do, step by step.
- **Example Data:** The data needed to complete the exercise.
- **Expected Output:** The calculation, table, chart, or explanation to produce.
- **Verification:** A way to check whether the result is reasonable or correct.

Exercises should begin with manageable tasks and gradually introduce more challenging ideas as the learning materials develop.

## Learning Roadmap

The initial learning materials are organized into five areas.

### 1. Financial Literacy

Understand the basic concepts and mechanics of financial markets.

Initial topics:

- Financial Markets and Market Participants
- Basic Stock Market Terminology
- Bid, Ask, and Spread
- Market Orders and Limit Orders
- Trading Volume and Liquidity
- Trading Costs
- Gross Returns vs. Net Returns

### 2. Quant Research

Learn how to formulate questions about financial markets and evaluate potential patterns using data.

Initial topics:

- Introduction to Quantitative Research
- Descriptive Statistics
- Probability and Expected Value
- Returns and Volatility
- Correlation
- Introduction to Time Series
- Alpha Fundamentals
- Trading Edge Fundamentals
- Signal and Noise
- Statistical Evidence

### 3. Quant Trading System

Understand how a research idea can be expressed as systematic trading rules and evaluated using historical data.

Initial topics:

- Strategy Rules and Trading Signals
- Entry and Exit Conditions
- Holding Period
- Introduction to Backtesting
- Basic Performance Measurement
- Introduction to Risk and Drawdown
- Transaction Costs
- Basic Strategy Validation

### 4. Programming for Quant

Develop programming skills through simple examples and financial data exercises.

Initial topics:

- Introduction to Programming
- Python Fundamentals
- Working with Tables and Data
- Introduction to NumPy and pandas
- Basic Data Cleaning
- Calculating Returns
- Creating Simple Charts
- Implementing Basic Research Calculations

Programming exercises should be introduced progressively. Learners can begin with conceptual examples and small calculations before working with more code.

### 5. AI for Quant Research

Learn how AI tools can support independent learning and research while understanding their limitations.

Initial topics:

- Introduction to Large Language Models (LLMs)
- Writing Clear Prompts
- Using AI to Understand Technical Concepts
- Research Assistance
- AI-Assisted Coding
- Checking Sources and Verifying Claims
- Understanding Hallucinations and Limitations
- Responsible Use of AI in Research

AI-generated explanations, calculations, and code should be checked rather than accepted without verification.

## How to Use This Repository

1. **Start with the basics.** Choose a topic that matches your current level.
2. **Understand the concept.** Read the explanation and examine the example.
3. **Practice.** Complete the exercise using the provided instructions and data.
4. **Check your work.** Compare your result with the expected output or verification method.
5. **Review the limitations.** Identify what the example demonstrates and what it does not establish.
6. **Continue learning.** Move to the next topic when you are ready.

You do not need to complete every topic in a fixed order. Some concepts build on one another, so revisit earlier materials whenever necessary.

## Learning Principles

- **Clarity before complexity:** Explain the basic idea before introducing more difficult material.
- **Examples before abstraction:** Use practical examples to connect concepts with real problems.
- **Practice alongside learning:** Give learners opportunities to apply what they understand.
- **Evidence before conclusions:** Evaluate claims using data and appropriate methods.
- **Verification before trust:** Check calculations, sources, and AI-generated outputs.
- **Limitations matter:** Explain assumptions and avoid conclusions that the evidence does not support.
- **Learn at your own pace:** Build understanding progressively rather than rushing through topics.

## Project Scope

The initial scope is intentionally focused on educational materials that can be published and maintained through GitHub.

The project will prioritize:

- Beginner-friendly explanations.
- Simple numerical and financial examples.
- Practical exercises with clear instructions.
- Small datasets where needed.
- Supporting Python examples when appropriate.
- Credible references and further reading.
- Thai and English educational resources as they become available.

The project will develop incrementally. Topics, examples, exercises, and supporting resources can be added or revised as the materials improve.

Advanced infrastructure, automated trading systems, and live brokerage integrations are outside the initial scope.

## Disclaimer

This project is for educational purposes only. It does not provide personalized investment advice, guaranteed trading signals, or assurances of profitability.

Historical examples and backtest results do not guarantee future performance. Trading involves risk, and actual results may differ from hypothetical calculations because of market conditions, execution, and transaction costs.

## Final Objective

The objective is to help people who are starting from zero build the knowledge and skills to understand quantitative trading, explore financial data, practice research methods, and evaluate ideas using evidence.

**Learn independently. Research systematically. Validate with evidence.**
